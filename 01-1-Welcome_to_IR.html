The North Korean nuclear program has existed, in some form or another, since the early 1960s.

  • The international community has repeatedly tried to convince the Kim family to give up their pursuit of nuclear weapons

  • Sometimes we have used “carrots”: Subsidized oil, food, economic aid, etc

  • Often we have used “sticks”: Sever economic sanctions


And, in sum, how effective have we been?

  • Anybody know how many nukes North Korea is estimated to have currently?

  • (Per the Arms Control Association:)

    • North Korea is estimated to have assembled 50 nuclear warheads, as of January 2024, and

    • They have enough fissile material for an estimated 70-90 MORE nuclear weapons



Making this choice has led North Korea to be cut off from basically all international trade and opportunities for growth.

  • The country is cartoonishly impoverished and the people outside the elite suffer massively.


Why on Earth would a country’s leadership choose to impoverish themselves in order to develop a weapon they couldn’t ever possibly use without inviting their own destruction?

Semester Outline: Section 4

What is the Future of Transnational Politics and IR?

Here’s the thing, most of the world’s international infrastructure was designed at the end of WWII

  • In 1945 we created the United Nations to maintain global peace

  • In 1946 we created the International Court of Justice to allow countries to settle disputes without violence or invasion

  • In 1944 we created the World Bank to reduce poverty and promote long-term economic development around the world

  • In 1947 we created the International Monetary Fund to promote global trade and economic growth


Basically all of these, and more, were founded 80 years ago in a VERY different era


In Section 4 of the class I want us to ask if 80 years later these institutions are still adapted well to the complexities of today

  • If not, what do we do about it?
